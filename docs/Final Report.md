# Final Report - UseOSM OSMF Microgrant

## 1. Executive Summary

UseOSM is an open, community-driven web platform created to make OpenStreetMap (OSM) data easier to discover, understand, and use. The project addresses a persistent gap in the OSM ecosystem: while millions of people contribute to mapping, many potential users, including students, NGOs, planners, developers, and decision-makers, remain unsure how to access OSM data or apply it to real-world problems.

During the project, the team successfully developed and launched the UseOSM website, bringing together real-world OSM use cases, tools and libraries, learning resources, community resources, and practical guidance in one accessible platform. The website provides a simple entry point for people who want to understand not only how OSM works, but also how its data can be applied beyond mapping.

The project also included several community-focused activities. A UseOSM town hall was organised to introduce the project, engage the OSM community, and gather feedback. This was followed by the UseOSM Map<>Kathon/Hackathon, which provided participants with an opportunity to explore and develop practical applications using OSM data. The hackathon was successfully completed, with four winning projects selected and announced. UseOSM was also presented at the State of the Map Global Conference in Paris, extending its reach to the wider international OSM community.

The project was supported by an OSMF microgrant of €4,862, which supported community outreach, research and content development, event activities, travel, domain costs, and contingencies.

Overall, UseOSM advances the OSM mission by shifting attention from "how to map" to "what can we do with the map." By making OSM easier to discover, understand, and apply, the project creates a practical bridge between OSM contributors and the wider community of people who can benefit from open geospatial data.

## 2. Project Background & Objectives

### 2.1 Background

Since 2015, our team has been consistent advocates for OpenStreetMap (OSM). We have organized mapathons, trained new contributors, and promoted OSM adoption across professional and community settings in Nigeria and beyond. Through this work, we noticed a recurring problem: while OSM contribution has grown steadily, far fewer people know how to actually use the data once it exists.

This became clear during the GIZ-funded Nigerian Energy Support Programme (NESPII), a national electrification planning project in which our team mapped thousands of businesses and facilities to support real infrastructure planning decisions. Collecting the data turned out to be the easier part. The harder task was convincing stakeholders, planners, government actors, and institutional partners to trust, adopt, and actually use the OSM data available to them.

This is not a one-off case. It's a pattern we keep seeing: students, NGOs, local governments, and planners often don't know OSM exists as an option, or don't know where to start. Even some active OSM contributors know how to map but not how the resulting data can be used in real decision-making. On top of that, existing tools and resources are scattered across wikis, forums, and documentation that assume prior geospatial knowledge.

The result is a gap between the data OSM produces and the people who could use it. OSM already has the data and the tools. What's missing is a simple bridge connecting them to the people who need them.

UseOSM was proposed to address this gap: a simple, community-driven platform to help NGO workers, students, local planners, developers, and other non-specialists discover, learn from, and apply OSM data. The original proposal centered on five core features: a plain-language explainer landing page with video, a curated directory of tools and libraries, real-world use cases, community-editable content via GitHub, and a lightweight website built with Astro and deployed via GitHub Pages and Actions.

### 2.2 Objectives and Expected Outcomes

UseOSM's main objective is to close the gap between contribution and usage by building one accessible place where people can discover, understand, and apply OSM data in real-world work.

**Core Objectives:**

- Lower the barrier to entry for understanding what OSM is and why it matters, using plain-language content aimed at non-specialists.
- Bring tools and libraries into one place, cutting down the time and expertise currently needed to find the right resource for a given task.
- Show practical value through real-world use cases, demonstrating how organizations and projects have applied OSM data to get concrete results.
- Build a resource the community owns by making all content editable via GitHub so people can keep it updated and translated over time.
- Keep the platform lightweight and easy to maintain, using Astro and GitHub Pages/Actions so it stays cheap and simple to run long-term.

**Expected Outcomes:**

- More OSM adoption in new sectors and regions, especially among NGOs, students, planners, and local governments who currently underuse open map data.
- Better visibility of the social and economic value of OSM, backed by documented, real-world use cases.
- Renewed motivation among existing contributors, who will be able to see the actual impact of their mapping work.
- A lasting, community-maintained entry point to OSM that keeps growing and gets translated well beyond the initial project team.

UseOSM is not meant to replace existing OSM tools or resources. It's meant to make them easier to find, easier to understand, and more widely used. By shifting the focus from "how to edit" to "why it matters," the project aims to turn visibility into usage, and usage into lasting value for OSM.

## 3. Implementation Summary

The UseOSM project was implemented through a combination of website development, content research and curation, community engagement, and outreach activities. While the original proposal anticipated a four-month implementation period beginning in June 2025, the actual delivery timeline shifted, with the main project activities taking place in 2026. Despite this adjustment, the core objectives of the project were completed, including the development and launch of the UseOSM website and the delivery of community engagement activities.

The project involved an iterative website development process beginning with UI/UX design in Figma, followed by development using Astro. Content research and website development were carried out in parallel, allowing the team to curate and organise real-world OSM use cases, tools and libraries, learning resources, and community information as the platform evolved. The final website was launched as a lightweight, accessible, and open-source platform, with the project repository available on GitHub to support future community contributions and maintenance.

Community engagement was a major part of the implementation. The team organised a UseOSM Town Hall on 15 July 2026, which introduced the Map<>Kathon, explained the requirements, supported team formation, and provided an open Q&A session. This was followed by the asynchronous Map<>Kathon build phase, initially scheduled to end on 14 August 2026. The build period was subsequently extended to allow participants additional time to complete and refine their projects. As a result of this adjustment, the judging period and final announcement were also moved, with judging taking place from 14–21 August and the winners announced on 25 August 2026, rather than the originally planned 24 August date.

The team also made a deliberate decision to focus on making existing OSM resources easier to discover and understand rather than developing another standalone mapping tool. This kept the project aligned with its core objective of promoting OSM usage and maximised the value of the available resources.

The project was additionally presented at the State of the Map Global Conference, providing an opportunity to share the project's outcomes with the wider international OSM community, receive further feedback, and identify opportunities for continued development and collaboration.

## 8. Challenges & Solutions

### 8.1 Obstacles Encountered During Implementation

**Timing of grant disbursement:** The original proposal planned a four-month implementation starting in June 2025. However, the grant funds were received in [month/year], later than expected. Activities that needed funding couldn't start on the original schedule. These included paid development work, use-case research support, event activities, and travel. As a result, most of the project's delivery moved into 2026.

**Extended development timeline:** Because of the funding delay, core site development stretched from July 2025 to August 2026 instead of the planned few months. Keeping momentum and team availability over that longer period was a challenge, especially since team members were also balancing other work commitments.

**Map<>Kathon timing:** During the asynchronous build phase, it became clear that many participants needed more time to finish and polish their projects than the original deadline of 14 August 2026 allowed. Keeping the original schedule would have meant fewer or weaker submissions.

**Scattered and uneven source material:** Researching use cases and tools was harder than expected. Information about how OSM data is used in practice is spread across wikis, forums, blog posts, project pages, and documentation. It varies a lot in quality and detail, and much of it assumes prior geospatial knowledge. Finding well-documented real-world examples took considerable time.

### 8.2 How the Team Adapted

**Working with what we could do without funds:** While waiting for disbursement, the team moved ahead with low-cost tasks. These included the project kickoff, the Figma prototype in July 2025, and early planning. When the funds arrived, we re-sequenced the remaining work. We ran content research and website development in parallel so we could recover time.

**Extending the Map<>Kathon:** We extended the build phase to give participants enough time. We also moved judging to 14–21 August and the winners announcement to 25 August 2026. We communicated these changes to participants early so nobody was caught off guard. The result was a completed hackathon with four winning projects.

**Using reliable sources and clear criteria:** For content research, we drew on established and actively maintained references. We also agreed on what a use case needed to include before being added. Our research support contributor helped with sourcing and documenting examples, and we reached our target of more than 10 documented use cases.

**Keeping the scope focused:** At several points it was tempting to build new features or a standalone tool. We decided to stay focused on making existing OSM resources easier to find and understand. This kept the project within budget and true to its purpose.

### 8.3 Lessons Learned

- **Plan for funding delays.** Future timelines should begin from the date funds are received rather than a fixed calendar date. They should also include a short buffer for administrative processes.
- **Have a "no-cost" track ready.** Having design and planning work that could move forward without funding meant the delay didn't stall the project entirely.
- **Build flexibility into community events.** Volunteer participants work around jobs, studies, and different time zones. Asynchronous formats need generous deadlines and room to extend them.
- **Content takes longer than code.** Curating good, trustworthy use cases was one of the most time-consuming parts of the project. Future phases should give research and documentation as much time and budget as development.
- **Set up measurements from day one.** Analytics and feedback collection should be in place before launch so impact can be tracked from the start rather than pieced together later.
- **A narrow focus is a strength.** Choosing to connect people to existing OSM tools rather than build a new one made the project easier to deliver and easier to maintain.

## 9. Sustainability & Maintenance Plan

The site is hosted on GitHub Pages with automated deployment via GitHub Actions. As a static site, it has low overhead, low cost, and no server infrastructure to manage. The domain (useosm.org) has already been purchased for the next 10 years.

Maintenance will be voluntary after the grant period ends. Because the site is static, ongoing upkeep is expected to be minimal, mainly reviewing community contributions and making occasional content updates. Clear documentation will be provided to support this, and feedback or issues will be handled through GitHub Issues.

The project is designed to be community-maintained. Contributions are accepted via GitHub, and content is sourced from established, actively updated references such as the OSM Wiki, WikiData, Taginfo, and other OSM-related sites. This reduces the amount of manual content work needed and keeps information current with little maintainer effort.

The static-site setup makes the project stable, fast, and cheap to run, which lowers the risk of it becoming unmaintainable over time. For long-term durability beyond the initial purchase, the team proposes renewing the useosm.org domain for an additional 9 years. Alternatively, they are open to moving to an OSM Foundation-supported subdomain (e.g., `useosm.openstreetmap.org`), which would raise visibility and remove future renewal costs. If approved, the budget would be reduced accordingly.

## 10. Future Development & Next Steps

UseOSM is designed to grow beyond its initial launch into a long-term, community-driven resource for discovering and using OpenStreetMap.

- **Planned improvements:** Expand the tools directory, add more real-world use cases, improve search and discovery, and introduce interactive learning resources.
- **Regional and language expansion with WikiTranslate or Locize:** Support more languages and highlight OSM applications and communities from underrepresented regions.
- **Quarterly community online meet-up:** Allow open discussions with the community on future development of the website and the community.
- **Integrate the OSM events calendar:** OpenStreetMap events, like meetups and mapping parties, can now be added to the site. An event page will be added so visitors have one clear place to see what's coming up within the OSM community.
- **Partnerships:** Collaborate with OSM communities, YouthMappers, HOT, universities, NGOs, developers, and other organisations working with open geospatial data.
- **Community growth:** Enable more users to contribute use cases, tools, translations, and learning resources through GitHub.

Our long-term vision is simple: make OSM easier to discover, understand, and use so that the value of the map goes far beyond those who create it.

## 11. Conclusion & Gratitude

UseOSM addresses an important gap in the OpenStreetMap ecosystem: the gap between contributing data and using it. While OSM has grown into a powerful global source of open geospatial data, many students, NGOs, planners, developers, journalists, and decision-makers still lack a simple way to discover what OSM data can do and how they can apply it.

By bringing together practical use cases, tools, guides, stories, and learning resources in one accessible, community-driven platform, UseOSM aims to make OSM easier to discover, understand, and apply. In doing so, it supports broader OSM adoption, demonstrates the social and economic value of open map data, and helps contributors see the real-world impact of their work.

Ultimately, UseOSM is not another mapping tool; it is an entry point to using the map that the community has built. It seeks to complement existing OSM tools and initiatives by answering a simple but important question: "Now that we've mapped it, what can we do with it?"

We sincerely thank the OpenStreetMap Foundation and the wider OSM community for promoting initiatives that strengthen the openness, accessibility, and impact of OpenStreetMap.

We are grateful for the opportunity to contribute to an ecosystem that has empowered communities around the world to create and share open geographic knowledge. The support of the OSMF would enable us to take the next step: helping more people understand the value of the data that OSM contributors have worked so hard to create.

UseOSM is intended to be built with the community, not simply for the community. We welcome OSM contributors, local communities, developers, researchers, organisations, and anyone interested in open geospatial data to contribute ideas, use cases, tools, feedback, translations, and other resources.

The project will remain open and community-driven through GitHub, the OSM Community Forum, community channels, and feedback sessions.

We invite the community to help us discover, document, and share the many ways OpenStreetMap is already making a difference around the world. Together, we can ensure that OSM is not only mapped, but understood, used, and valued by an even wider community. Thank you for considering UseOSM and for helping us build the next chapter of the OpenStreetMap story.
